## 1. Retail preparation before advertising

When a used van first enters stock, it is prepared for retail presentation before being advertised. This includes:

- valet;
- polish;
- paint correction where required;
- obvious faults being dealt with before the vehicle is presented for sale.

Examples of obvious faults that would be dealt with before advertising include an engine-management warning light or a tyre that is clearly unsuitable for retail sale.

This is distinct from the deeper point-of-sale mechanical preparation carried out after the vehicle is sold.

## 2. Point-of-sale workshop preparation

The main mechanical preparation happens after sale. Once a vehicle is sold, it joins the workshop preparation queue and undergoes a 101-point inspection / PDI.

The 101-point process covers, among other areas:

- mechanical condition;
- electrical checks;
- bodywork;
- tyres;
- brakes;
- warning lights;
- cambelts and timing belts where relevant;
- wet belts where relevant;
- review of previous MOT failures and advisories.

A sold vehicle receives a new 12-month MOT and servicing at point of sale.

## 3. Servicing policy

Every sold vehicle receives at least an oil-and-filter service.

A full service is carried out when:

- the vehicle is due one based on its service history;
- there is no service history; or
- there is no sufficiently recent service history to support relying on previous servicing.

The absence of adequate recent service evidence therefore results in a more comprehensive service rather than an assumption that servicing has already been completed.

## 4. Ford wet-belt policy

For relevant Ford commercial vehicles, Van Finance Company generally replaces the wet belt at point of sale regardless of mileage or older service history.

Models specifically identified by the owner include:

- Ford Transit;
- Ford Transit Custom;
- Ford Transit Connect;
- Ford Transit Courier.

The normal exception is where documented history confirms that the wet belt was replaced within the previous 1,000 miles or within the previous 6 months.

The reason for this policy is the known concern around wet-belt issues on these vehicles.

A wet-belt or cambelt replacement can sometimes add roughly one or two days to preparation, but this is not an automatic delay on every vehicle.

## 5. Cambelt / timing-belt policy on other vehicles

Cambelts and timing belts are replaced in line with manufacturer recommendations.

If a vehicle has no service history, Van Finance Company will automatically replace the cambelt / timing belt where applicable rather than assume that the work has already been completed.

## 6. Tyres, brakes and MOT-history review

Tyres and brakes are checked during the PDI / 101-point process.

Van Finance Company does not aim to hand a vehicle over with tyres sitting close to the legal minimum. Where a tyre is approaching the legal limit, it is replaced so the vehicle is handed over with a good usable tread depth rather than merely being technically legal.

Brakes are checked as part of the same process.

The workshop also reviews the previous MOT history, including failures and advisories, to make sure relevant issues have been addressed before handover.

## 7. Standard warranty

Every sold vehicle includes a standard in-house warranty for 3 months or 3,000 miles, whichever limit is reached first under the applicable warranty terms.

Customers may also have the option to purchase an upgraded warranty.

The standard warranty is handled directly by Van Finance Company rather than requiring the customer to deal with a third-party warranty administrator.

If a warranty issue develops:

- the customer contacts Van Finance Company;
- the vehicle can normally be taken to a garage local to the customer;
- a garage the customer already knows and trusts is preferred where practical;
- the after-sales team deals directly with the garage;
- authorised warranty repair costs are paid directly by Van Finance Company to the garage.

The customer does not normally need to return the vehicle to Van Finance Company simply because a repair is required.

## 8. After-sales department

Van Finance Company has a dedicated after-sales function for customers who experience an issue while their vehicle is under warranty.

Customers should contact the business directly by email or WhatsApp and, where possible, provide photos or videos so the team has useful information from the outset.

A member of the after-sales team will then contact the customer and work to resolve the issue as quickly as reasonably possible.

If workshop attention is needed, a local garage is normally the quickest and most practical option. If a vehicle genuinely needs to return to Van Finance Company, recovery can be arranged. Where required and available, a courtesy vehicle can be supplied during repairs.

Repeat business and long-term customer relationships are important to Van Finance Company, so after-sales support is treated as part of the overall customer experience rather than as an external warranty-company handoff.

## 9. AA breakdown cover

Sold vehicles are signed up for 12 months of basic AA breakdown cover from the delivery or handover date.

Van Finance Company's practical recommendation is that customers should still consider suitable paid / premium breakdown cover through their insurer or another provider, because a basic complimentary policy is not the same as a more comprehensive paid policy.

Do not market the basic cover as if it provides every feature available under a premium breakdown product.

## 10. Remote-buying process

Van Finance Company's intended customer experience is to make remote van buying simple and low-friction.

Typical journey:

1. Customer is approved for finance or receives an available credit limit.
2. If they have not already chosen a vehicle, they choose one from the website.
3. Van Finance Company prepares a quote for that vehicle.
4. If the customer wishes to proceed, the finance-document link is sent digitally.
5. The customer completes the finance documentation online.
6. The customer pays a £100 reservation deposit.
7. Once that £100 is received, the vehicle is officially taken off sale.
8. The vehicle enters the workshop queue for MOT, servicing and the 101-point inspection / PDI.
9. After workshop preparation, the vehicle goes through the valet process ready for delivery.
10. Van Finance Company contacts the customer to arrange delivery to the customer's home or work address.
11. If there is a remaining customer deposit balance, it is due the day before delivery and can be paid by card over the phone or by bank transfer. Payment instructions are included in the delivery-confirmation email.
12. The vehicle is delivered directly to the customer.

Delivery is free under the current Van Finance Company proposition.

Typical turnaround is 7–10 working days and is often around a week, subject to workshop requirements, parts, MOT, belt replacement and other preparation needs. This is a typical timeframe, not a guaranteed delivery deadline.

## 11. Delivery paperwork

At delivery, the customer receives the relevant vehicle paperwork and the green V5C/2 new-keeper slip required for the vehicle-registration / taxing process.

The owner described the replacement V5C as normally arriving in the customer's name after delivery. Do not promise a fixed DVLA arrival time as a Van Finance Company guarantee.

## 12. Final delivery-driver inspection

The workshop's 101-point sign-off is followed by a second check by the delivery driver on the day before or day of delivery.

The driver carries out a walk-around / secondary inspection covering items including:

- bodywork and visible damage;
- dashboard warning lights;
- mileage;
- keys;
- paperwork;
- general readiness for delivery.

This gives the vehicle two separate pre-handover checks: workshop sign-off and the delivery driver's secondary inspection.

## 13. If a remote customer is unhappy on delivery

If a remotely purchasing customer reports that the vehicle is not what they expected or raises a problem on delivery, the first route is the after-sales team.

The customer should send as much information as possible, ideally including photos or video. The after-sales team will assess the issue and look to rectify it promptly.

Nine times out of ten, the owner considers a local garage to be the faster and more practical route where repair work is required. If the vehicle genuinely needs to return, Van Finance Company can arrange recovery. Where required and available, a courtesy vehicle can be supplied while repairs are carried out.

This operational after-sales process is separate from a customer's statutory cancellation or rejection rights.

## 14. Remote-purchase customer protection: compliance wording

Customers frequently ask, “What if I'm not happy with the vehicle when it is delivered?”

Approved internal rule for Jasmine/content:

- A qualifying consumer distance sale can carry statutory cancellation rights that differ from an on-premises purchase.
- A regulated distance credit agreement can also have a 14-day cancellation / withdrawal right under applicable FCA rules.
- These are not the same legal right.
- Do not state that cancelling the finance agreement automatically cancels or rejects the vehicle sale.
- Do not promise a blanket full refund in every remote-purchase scenario without checking the applicable contract, use/condition of the vehicle and legal basis.
- For customer-specific legal questions, explain the general position and refer the customer to their agreement / relevant team rather than improvising legal advice.

Authoritative references checked 16 August 2026:

- GOV.UK, Online and distance selling: https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/distance-selling
- GOV.UK, Accepting returns and giving refunds: https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds
- FCA Handbook, CONC 11 Cancellation: https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/CONC/11/

## 15. Used electric-van guidance

The owner identifies battery condition as a major consideration on older / early-generation used electric vans.

A used electric van that is five or six years old should not be assumed to retain the same usable range that was quoted when it was new.

Van Finance Company's practical buyer guidance is to ask the dealer for a battery-health check when considering a used electric van.

Do not invent a universal degradation percentage or battery-life figure.

## 16. What VFC can safely say about how commercial-vehicle buyers choose vans

The owner does not claim to know a universal psychology for why customers choose one van over another.

What can be said from experience:

- many commercial-vehicle buyers already know the broad type of vehicle their work requires;
- common starting categories include small vans, medium vans, long-wheelbase vans, Lutons, tippers, dropsides and pickups;
- suitability for the customer's business is usually central;
- monthly payment can influence the final decision, but VFC does not claim it is always the primary reason.

Do not manufacture claims about “the most common customer mistakes” or precise buyer motivations without supporting data.

## 17. VFC remote-buying ethos

The operating philosophy is to make buying a van remotely simple and hassle-free from start to finish.

The customer-facing journey can be summarised as:

Choose a van → pay the reservation deposit → sign finance documentation digitally → VFC prepares the van → free delivery to the customer's door.

The simplicity of the process, local-garage warranty support, after-sales help and free delivery are all intended to reduce the friction of buying a commercial vehicle remotely.

The owner reports that customers regularly comment on how easy the process is and that the business receives repeat customers. Treat that as owner-supplied qualitative experience, not as a quantified customer-satisfaction statistic unless supporting review / CRM data is available.
